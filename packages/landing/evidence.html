How a wake works

  1. Point and pinch. Quest Space Setup (or the emulator room) already holds furniture-sized boxes. A hand ray draws a dotted outline and a label chip. Pinch, or click in the browser.
  2. A face blooms. On the side that faces you, in its upper third, 1 cm proud of the surface, held by a WebXR anchor. It blinks, follows you, and turns at most 40° toward you.
  3. Poke, pet, flick. A fingertip poke makes it jump. A slow palm stroke makes it purr. A flick sends it spinning and grumpy. In the browser: tap, rub, swipe.
  4. Wake a second. With a key, Muse Spark writes a persona from the label and the neighbours, and ask_object lets them gossip (at most two hops). Without a key you type both lines. Puppet mode, labelled.

How to open it

  1. No headset. pnpm gateway, then pnpm --filter app dev, then open https://localhost:8091/. The room is labelled “Browser diorama, not your room”. Click a piece of furniture.
  2. Desktop emulator. On that same page, “Enter mixed reality (emulated)” uses IWER’s synthetic room. Hands only.
  3. Quest 3 / 3S. Run Space Setup so the furniture is labelled, open the app in the Quest browser, enter mixed reality, point and pinch. Not run on a headset yet. Steps: docs/QUEST.md.

Questions

Can it wake a mug?

Not in the headset. The room model holds furniture-sized boxes from Space Setup. Small things need camera access, which Quest Browser does not reliably give a web app yet.

Where does my voice go?

To the Yadori gateway, which sends it to Muse Voice Transcribe. No audio is stored. The spirits’ voices are made on the gateway.

Does the face stick to the couch?

It is placed on the couch’s detected box and fixed with a WebXR anchor. In the emulator the face centre sat on the computed target within 1 cm (measured 0.00 cm).

Is the key ever in the browser?

No. Only the gateway reads MODEL_API_KEY. The app talks to /api.
